Two-Post Lift Power Unit

The whole lift comes down to one column-mounted assembly: an electric motor bolted to a gear pump, sitting on a plastic reservoir. Twelve parts, two circuits. Here is what each piece does and what it does when it fails.

Rated capacity
9,000 lb
Motor
2 HP, 220 V, 1-ph
Reservoir
~3 US gal

~2,700 psi
Fluid
AW-32 or ATF
Restart interval
15 s minimum

When it acts up

Hums, will not turn

Start capacitor, nearly always. A motor that hums is receiving power and failing to start, which is a different repair from a motor that is silent.

Dead after several quick cycles

Thermal overload. The decal asks for fifteen seconds between starts for this reason. Give it twenty minutes and it comes back on its own.

Slow or jerky rise

Low fluid or air in the system. Top off with the lift all the way down, then cycle it to full height three times with no load to bleed it.

Drifts down overnight

The check valve in the manifold or the cylinder seals. Not the hose, and not the release lever unless it was left cracked open.

Values here are typical for this class of unit. The capacity plate on the machine reads 9,000 lb, and the decal on the switch box calls for fifteen seconds between starts. Everything else, motor rating, fluid type, relief setting, comes off the data plate on your own motor and the manual for your lift model. Always rest the lift on its mechanical safety locks before anyone goes underneath.
